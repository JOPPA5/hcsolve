## My reading, sign by sign

1. line1: i (no dot, shaped like the "u" of "uten" in body line 6), s, t, e, n -> "isten"
2. line2: v, e, l, e, t -> "velet" (= veled; same spelling habit as the body "velet" in "isten velet viszont")
3. line3: c (looped like e), s, o, k, then a solid round ink dot, then l, a, k -> "csok.lak". The dot stands where a second o is expected, giving "csok[o]lak" = csókollak (the double l is missing in the body too, where it is written "csokalak").
4. line4: I (looped capital), l, o, n, k (blotted), a -> "Ilonka"

This matches the solver's literal reading exactly (isten / velet / csok.lak / Ilonka). The normalized form "Isten veled, csókollak, Ilonka" and the translation "God be with you, I kiss you, Ilonka" are correct. The dot is disclosed, and treating it as the o is the single-letter restoration that makes a word, as the standards allow.

## Type

The computed task asked whether the type change to "MASC + Steganography" is justified. The solver did not make that change. SOLUTION.md argues against it: the card has no substitution cipher anywhere, and the stamp-box text is plain Hungarian. I agree. All the signs in the box are ordinary Latin cursive. None of them are Jolanka cipher signs. The steganographic part (writing hidden under the stamp) is real. The MASC part is not supported for this card. "MASC + Steganography" would overstate it. The solver's label is "Steganography (plain text under the stamp), no cipher". If the register needs one of its existing labels, it should be a steganography-only label, not one that contains MASC. This card has no accepted solution and no register entry. That is consistent with the card being PARTIAL.
