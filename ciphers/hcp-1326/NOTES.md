### What is on the card (plain, unenciphered)
- Postmark: Quebec, DEC 7, 7:30 AM, 1908. 7 Dec 1908 was a Monday; the writer heads the card "Sunday."
- Address: Miss F. M. Owen / 44 Stedman St / Brookline / Mass.
- Instruction, lower left: "Read the above as / shown by the arrows. / and you will you / will know that it / is so. [paraph]". "you will you will" is the writer's own repetition.
- Box beside the grid: "x x x X" (three small, one large) over an oval paraph. Same paraph after "is so.". It looks like R H M or similar; the letters are not certain, so it is reported as a monogram only. The x's are presumably kisses (interpretation).

### Hypotheses tried; none proved
1. Simple substitution or any letter cipher: rejected. 4 shapes, 16 tokens, no repetition usable for language statistics.
2. Morse by size (small = dot, large = dash): every row gives ...- = V, so "VVVV". Columns 1-3 also give ...-, because the row-4 bars are tall. The 3 x 3 small / outer large symmetry is real, but "V" gives no message, and the word "it is so" is not explained. Rejected as a reading; symmetry noted. Reading the shapes as Morse (dots, ticks, bars) gives repeats, no text.
3. L-path as a count: 3 small then 4 large. "1-4-3" = "I love you" is a period-compatible idea (from memory, Minot's Ledge light, Massachusetts; not checked), and the card suits a love message, but the signs give 3-4 or 3-1, not 1-4-3. Speculation only.
4. Rebus by naming shapes (star, dot, dash/tick, bar/block, "square"), or a proverb about many small things and a large one, or a compliment ("a star", "square"): any number of sentences can be made and nothing on the card selects one. Not adopted, per the rule against forcing words onto noise.

### Why it stops
The card's own text says the block expresses a statement ("it is so"), so it is probably a private pictorial rebus or love-token puzzle rather than a cipher, and it may depend on a joke shared by the writer and Miss Owen. Its meaning is underdetermined: 16 signs from 4 shapes carry no checkable redundancy, so no proposed reading can be tested. All of the card's ordinary writing is read; the symbol message is not.
