Assessment: the signs are composites of Pigpen-like outlines (`⊔`, `⊓`, `V`),
digits (3, 4, 9) and stroke counts (I, II, III). This resembles the
outline+numeral Pigpen family (hcp-1193) and the outline+dot-count family
(hcp-1131/1189/1190), but the numerals here exceed 3 (4, 9) and strokes are
combined, so that key does not transfer directly. Tested informally: reading
stroke counts (III) as the block selector of the hcp-1193 alphabet gives no
consistent letters, because most composites cannot be decomposed uniquely.
There are only ~12–15 composite signs, roughly a third partly hidden by the
watermark; no sister card with the same script exists in this repo (searched
for Neale / Tuebrook / Birkenhead and the Pigpen-family solutions).

Why it stops: too little text, no reliable segmentation into signs, several
signs physically obscured by the watermark at 1024 px resolution, and no
known key of the same family. Any letter assignment would be forced onto
noise. A full-resolution, unwatermarked scan (or a sister card from the same
sender to A. J. Neale) would be needed. No SOLUTION.md written; no marker.
