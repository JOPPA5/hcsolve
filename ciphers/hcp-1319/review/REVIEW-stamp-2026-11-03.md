## My reading, sign by sign

Line 1: `c s o` + accent stroke + `k o l o` + final sign.
- c, s, o: clear. The accent is a separate curved stroke above, between o and k, so it reads as ó.
- k: clear, a looped k like the one in "Árokszállás" in the address.
- o: inside the ink blot, but its loop is visible.
- l: tall loop, clear. o: small closed loop, clear.
- Final sign: top bar, diagonal down to the left, a foot, then a long descender curving down and to the right. By shape it is a z. It has no humps, so it is not a formed m.

Line 2: four signs and a flourish.
- 1: an arc that rises from lower left, goes over the top and comes down to a small curl at the baseline. It has no crossbar and does not descend, unlike the J of "Jász" in the address. S is the best fit; J cannot be excluded.
- 2: a tall stroke rising into a top hook, then coming down to a foot. It is z-like, like the z after S in "Szép…".
- 3: a dark closed a with a high stem. **No accent above it.** The space above is clean.
- 4: a small hook, then a long descent below the baseline with a zig (a z-foot), ending in a long rising flourish to the right. It is z with a descender; a y is the only other shape possible.

I read `Szaz` + flourish, i.e. *száz* with the accent not written. Nothing follows the flourish, and there is no "-szor".

## Differences (all minor)

1. **The k is not under the blot.** SOLUTION.md and the report say "the k and first o are under an ink blot". In `l1a_5x.png` the k stands completely clear, to the left of the blot. Only the first o (and the foot of the l) lies in the blot. Fix the wording.
2. **The headline overstates line 1.** The final sign is fully visible and has the shape of a z, so *m* is a reading by sense of a visible malformed letter, not a restoration of a hidden one. The text discloses this, but the normalized line and the report's headline give a clean "Csókolom." It should be marked, e.g. *Csókolo[m?]* or "Csókolom (final letter z-shaped, read m by sense)".
3. **There is no accent on the a of line 2.** The "literal reading" gives `Száz?`. The ink has no accent over the a (`l2_3x_rgb.png`), so the literal reading is `Szaz`, normalized *Száz*. Say so.
4. **The final letter of line 2 has one more alternative.** By shape, the last sign could be y as well as z; it is not only a matter of z with a descender. This does not change the reading, which is tentative anyway.

## What I could not verify

- Why the final sign of line 1 is z-shaped (a slip, or an unusual m) cannot be decided from this card alone. The sister cards' stamp notes in the same hand would be the test.
