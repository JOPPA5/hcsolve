## Cipher type and key

Hungarian open text with the salutation and the confidential passage in a digit cipher (register label: MASC). Each letter is one sign: a digit, or a digit with a mark. The comma, dot or colon belongs to the sign. There are no word-division marks beyond small gaps. The family key comes from the FINAL cards hcp-1174, 1178 and 1179; `4.` = gy and `6` = j are new on this card, and `4` = f confirms the value that hcp-1179 gave as tentative.

## Plaintext (Hungarian; [ ] = enciphered on the card; punctuation in the cipher part is mine)

[Kedves igazszívű Margitkám]!

Szerencsésen haza érkeztem, nagyon meleg volt és nagy sár. Gyalog jöttem a telepen lejjebb egy darabig, ott ért utól Nagy Lojzi, egy lovas kocsival jött és felvett. Úgy értem aztán haza nyolc órakor. Már a lapokat is megvettem, de nem volt akitől elküldjem. Már az arcképet is berámáztattam, ami othol maradt neked arckép, annak majd magam fogok faragott rámát csinálni. Más különös ujság nincs. Róza nem jött el. Majd ha irsz, sokat irjál mindenről. Most már [irhatsz akármit, ne félj, úgyse] lássa [meg senki. Ha betüvel nem, akkor irjál számokkal. Bizhatsz bennem testvér, én úgy bizok benned, mint a jó istenkében.]

Sokszor csókollak, maradok [hű kedvesed] ([Árpád])

Mezőkövesd, 1915. XII/28.

Address: Tek. / Rózsareményű / Sziber Margitka / kisasszonynak / Szomolya / u. p. Bogács. Postmark Mezőkövesd, DEC 28; the year is hidden, the written date gives 1915. Printed on the card: Serie Nr. 2963 and a trefoil trademark.

## English translation

The brackets in both translations are approximate around `lássa ... meg`: the clear verb and its enciphered prefix form one idea, so the exact division is shown only in the Hungarian above.

[Dear true-hearted Margitka]! I got home safely; it was very warm and there was a lot of mud. I came on foot along the settlement and further down for a while, and there Nagy Lojzi caught up with me; he was coming with a horse carriage and picked me up. So I reached home at eight o'clock. I have already bought the postcards too, but there was nobody to send them with. I have also had the portrait framed; as for the portrait for you that stayed at home, I will make a carved frame for it myself. There is no other special news. Róza did not come. When you write, write a lot about everything. Now [you can write anything, do not be afraid, nobody will] see it [anyway. If not with letters, then write with numbers. You can trust me, sister; I trust you as I trust the good Lord.] ("Sister" renders `testvér`, a plain turn of address here: see Uncertainties 5.) Many kisses, I remain [your faithful sweetheart] ([Árpád]). Mezőkövesd, 28 December 1915.

## Command

Run from the repo root:

    python3 -I ciphers/hcp-1156/work/decode.py

It checks the SHA-256 of all four scans, decodes every sign with the table, asserts that each word matches the stated reading (reduced to what the signs show, since the writer often leaves accents out), and prints the full text and address. The open text is hard-coded in the script.

## Uncertainties

Cipher signs:

1. The first sign of `számokkal` (block line 4) is inside an ink blot. A crossbar and a dot at lower right survive, which fits `7.`; the letter sz is a restoration by word sense, the only one in the cipher part.
2. `Margitkám`: the `1'` has its mark joined to the stem as a short bar at upper right, not a free high dot. Read as á by the name.
3. `irjál` and `számokkal`, `akármit` show `1.` (a) for á. That is the writer's spelling, not a key change; `1'` appears only in Margitkám and Árpád.
4. `irhatsz`: the `5` has a small tick to its left, taken as a pen mark. `1,` of `benned` and the `6,` of `-ben` (the last, over the printed "Serie") are heavily inked; the commas are visible. The comma of `7,` in `istenkében` is faint.
5. `testvér` is clear in the signs. Literally "sibling"; "sister" is my translation for an address to Margitka, and the word could be a turn of phrase. I do not take it as family evidence.
6. `4.` = gy occurs twice on the card, in `úgyse` (block line 2) and `úgy` (line 5). It has no attestation on other cards; it rests on those two words, which leave no alternative. The clear `úgy` of the open text is not cipher evidence. `4` = f occurs once (`félj`) and also rests on the tentative value of hcp-1179. `6` = j occurs three times (`félj`, `irjál`, `jó`).
7. `:1` in both `gy` words has an unusual form, and these two readings are helped by word sense. In `igazszívű`, `betüvel` and `hű` it shows two separate dots. In `úgy` the mark is a single slanted bar left of and parallel to the stem; in `úgyse` it is a heavy parallel stroke joined to the stem at the top. I read it as the colon dragged into a line. It is certainly not a plain `1`.
8. `senki`: the first sign's hook touches the crossbar and makes a small closed triangle, so it resembles a closed 4 at low enlargement. The bar runs through the stem to the right as in the other 7s, unlike the `4,` of `meg` just before it, and `g-enki` is no word. Read `7,`.

Open text:

- `Nagy Lojzi`: the capital N is a looped form that could look like T, and the L is a looped capital. Reported as the reading that makes a name; the person is not otherwise known.
- `ért`: the card seems to have `értt`, with a doubled t under one crossbar. `utól` is as written for standard `utol`.
- `telepen`: the last letters touch the printed divider; the sense of "a telepen lejjebb" in the translation is approximate.
- `othol` is as written (colloquial for `otthon`). `berámáztattam` is broken across a line, as `berámáz-tattam`.
- Sentence division in the open text is mine; the writer uses few stops.

## Why I believe it is correct

- The table comes from the FINAL sister cards and was not fitted to this text, apart from three values that this card itself fixes: `6` = j by three clean words, `4.` = gy by two, and `4` = f by one word plus the tentative value on hcp-1179.
- 151 signs decode into continuous, grammatical Hungarian that runs through the clear word `lássa` and is a natural sequel of the clear `Most már`.
- The passage names its own method: "if not with letters, then write with numbers".
- `ne félj`, `irjál` and `úgy bizok benned` are not forced: only the signs on the card are used, and `irjál` also occurs in clear on the same card a few lines above.
- The signature `Árpád` is the same sign string as on hcp-1174; the address, hand and town match that correspondence.
