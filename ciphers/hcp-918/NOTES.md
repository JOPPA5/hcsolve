## 2026-11-03 — Claude Opus 5.5 (solver): no cipher on this card, returned partial

Read both originals in work/hires/ (message side rotated 90 degrees, crops in work/crops/).

Address side: J. R. Clements, Esq., Box 88, Binghamton, N.Y.; postmark Washington D.C., Sep 27 3 PM 86;
printed 1-cent US postal card (the stamp is a printed indicium, so nothing can be hidden under it). No other writing found.

Message side is ordinary plain English in Latin script, dated in red "Sep. 27/86." and signed
"James S. McGee, Washin., D.C.". It is a letter to the editor of an amateur puzzle department
("Friend Dick Ens"?: the salutation's second word is uncertain), sending answers to numbered puzzles:

- 406 W-h-eat (beheadment), 409 On-e, 412 To-morrow (413 to 416 marked with ditto marks)
- 407 a word diamond/rhomboid (U / ARM / ARSIS / BUTES / LINES / GATE / TOR / FF / S), credited "Festus"
- 408 a form (YARRISH / DUARTE / DDIES / IANT / ...) and 410 INBRED... with red overlay words
  (IMPEL..., EINES, OSTERS, ...): word-square or rhomboid answers
- 411: "411 must have gone on a 'tear'" (puzzle 411 apparently defective)
- 417 JASPER ... a word rhomboid with red overlay, credited "Festus" and "= Stivers"
- Running text: "Where, Oh, where are the prize offers for September! I shall do my best to send a ch[eck?]
  for the month, and hope to see your announcements in on the 30." If J. M. downs F. he will have to
  get up very early in the morning."

"Festus" and "Stivers" are puzzlers' pen names (typical of 1880s amateur-journalism puzzle columns).
The letter-blocks are word-puzzle (diamond / square / rhomboid) solutions, not enciphered text:
every row is a plain English word or proper noun. There is no substitution, transposition, code or
hidden writing on the card. The portal lists it under the Schrödel cryptographic postcards because of
the puzzle grids.

Status: no ciphertext to decipher. No DONE marker, no SOLUTION.md, no register entry.
