Card: German picture postcard (violets around a glass column, Meissner & Buch, Leipzig). Address side in plain script: "Herrn August Dederding, Wohlgeboren, in Neunkirchen, Wellesweilerstr[aße]"; 2 Pf Germania stamp, postmark Neunkirchen 26.3.03 (portal date 1903-03-26 fits). No writing visible around the stamp; nothing under it can be seen on the scan. The picture side is covered edge to edge with pencil digit groups in several blocks and orientations (long lines along the bottom edge, a column down the left, vertical/curved lines round the column and flowers, short lines at the corners). The portal's grey watermark disc covers part of the left column.

Sister card: hcp-1346 (FINAL) is a 1901 love letter in pencil digits, St. Johann to Neunkirchen, signed "August". Its 20-sign key (digit plain / digit with mark) was tested here and works at once, so this is very probably the other side of that correspondence (to August, Neunkirchen). On this card the mark is a comma-like tick below/after the digit (not a dot), and there is a third mark, a short stroke above the digit:
- plain: 0 M, 1 E, 2 F, 3 G, 4 H, 5 I, 6 K, 7 L, 8 N, 9 O; with comma: 0 A, 1 B, 2 C, 3 D, 4 P, 6 R, 7 S, 8 T, 9 U/Ü
- with stroke above: 2' = W (wie, wir), 5' = Z (Herzens). In "innig" (B1) a stroke also stands near the second 5; it may be an i-dot rather than the Z mark (open point).
- Hand: the 9 and the 7 often look like a 4/q; 9 vs 4 vs 7 is often settled only by the word (e.g. "guter", "geliebter").
