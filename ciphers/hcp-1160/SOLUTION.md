# hcp-1160 solution (round 1, revised after review 1)

Solver: Claude Opus 5.5, 2026-11-02. No earlier work on this number. The portal shows Not solved and attaches no solution or transcription. No published solution of this card was found in the repo. The HistoCrypt article the portal links was not checked for this card, so I cannot say whether it discusses it. The transcription is my own, from `work/hires/`.

## Card

Picture postcard (a couple with roses), Mezőkövesd, 11 November 1915, signed Sz. A., to Sziber Margitka, Szomolya, u. p. Bogács, Borsod m. Language Hungarian. Almost the whole message side is enciphered: 351 signs, plus one cancelled word. Only one note at the foot is open. A separate message stands in violet pencil across the top of the address half.

## Cipher type and key

Register label: MASC. One sign per Hungarian letter, a digit alone or a digit plus a mark, with no word division. The key was transferred from the FINAL cards hcp-1174, 1178, 1179 and the cards hcp-1170, 1172; the signs absent from those tables were fixed from words on this card and agree with sister cards (below). All 32 signs in the table occur on this card.

The pattern is regular: for n, g, t, l the comma gives the plain letter and the dot the palatal (`6,`/`6.`, `4,`/`4.`, `8,`/`8.`, `5,`/`5.`); `7.` and `9.` follow as the digraphs sz and zs.

Values not on the three key cards, with the sister cards that agree (FINAL: hcp-1164, 1166; the others are still in review, so they are unreviewed support):
- `5.` = ly: only in `melynek`, once. Same value in the same word on hcp-1151, and on hcp-1142 (`amelynek`) and FINAL hcp-1164 (`milyen`, `olyankor`, `oly`). The family is not uniform: hcp-1178 and 1179 file `5.` as l, and FINAL hcp-1166 keeps it as l in `helet` with ly as the open alternative. On this card it stands where ly belongs. One token cannot settle the family question.
- `8.` = ty: only in `hattyúdalom`, once, directly after `8,`. Same value in `kártya` on FINAL hcp-1164 and 1166 and on hcp-1142, 1146, 1147, 1151.
- `9.` = zs: only in `rózsáról`, once. Same value on FINAL hcp-1164 (`rózsák`) and hcp-1137; the rhyme and the simile fix the word here.
- `4` = f: in `fel`, `fénykép`, `fiúval`, `fe(l)viszem`. It was marked "appears to be" on hcp-1179.
- `.1` = u: `tudhatod`, `hullok`, `pusztán`, `hattyú`, `fiúval`; agrees with hcp-1172. On this card u and ú are not told apart.

## Plaintext (Hungarian; [ ] = enciphered on the card)

Punctuation, capitals and word division in the cipher passages are mine. Lengths on ó, í, ú are supplied, since the signs do not mark them.

[Kedves Margitkám]! [A lapodat megkaptam, melynek nagyon megörültem. Kedves Margitkám, tudhatod hogy ha csak lehet fel megyek, de ha nagyon rosz üdő lesz nem mehetek.]

I
[Elhervadok, porba hullok,
mint rózsáról a szirom,
nem ejt könnyet senki értem,
pusztán áll majd a sírom.]

II
[Az erdei kis dalosok
zengik el hattyúdalom ...
Egy bohó szegény fiúval
kevesebb lesz, mondhatom.]

ha esetleg rosz üdő lenne és nem mehetnék [bocsás meg] ) [számtalanszor csókollak] ) Mindnyájatokat csókollak.

1915. XI/11. Sz. A.

Violet pencil, top of the address half, set off by a ruled line (all enciphered):
[a fénykép készevan, pénszt külgyél le, majd kiváltom és feviszem]

Address (open): Szépreménnyü / Sziber Margitka / kisasszonynak / Szomolya / u. p. Bogács. / Borsod m.

Normal spellings: Szépreménnyü = Szépreményű, rosz = rossz, üdő = idő, bocsás = bocsáss, pénszt = pénzt, külgyél = küldjél, készevan = készen van, feviszem = felviszem, porba hullok as written. The two brackets `)` are on the card and act as dividers.

## English translation

Dear Margitka! I received your card, which made me very glad. Dear Margitka, you may know that if it is at all possible I will come up, but if the weather is very bad I cannot come.

I. I wither, I fall into the dust, like the petal from a rose; no one sheds a tear for me, my grave will stand desolate.

II. The little songsters of the forest sing my swan song out... With one foolish poor boy there will be fewer, I can say.

If the weather should perhaps be bad and I could not come, forgive me. Countless times I kiss you. I kiss you all. 11 November 1915. Sz. A.

Top note: The photograph is ready (`készevan`, read as `készen van`); send down money, then I will redeem it and bring it up.

## Command

Run from the repo root:

    python3 -I ciphers/hcp-1160/work/decode.py

It checks the SHA-256 of all four scans, decodes `work/transcription.txt` with the table, asserts each row against the stated reading, and prints the raw decoding and the full text. The formatted full text and the open note are typed in the script, so only the raw rows are computed. It needs no third-party module. Re-run after the review fixes: hashes OK, 351 signs, 32 distinct, 349 by the table and 2 by sense, all rows match.

## Uncertainties

Two of the 351 signs depend partly on word sense (`ODD` in `work/decode.py`):

1. `üdő` in the prose: one dot stands free left of the 1. A bump on the left of the stem sits where the upper dot of `:1` falls in `megörültem` (`review/work/z_udo.png`, `z_mego.png`), so the second dot is likely but fused with the stem, not proven. `üdő` stands open in the foot note and elsewhere in the family.
2. `könnyet`, the sign after `6,`: the digit 6 is clear, but its mark lies in an ink blot whose outline has the head and tail of a comma (`review/work/z_konny.png`). The literal reading therefore leans to `6,`, a spelling `könnet`. I print `könnyet` as a word-sense restoration: the y may have been a dot under the blot or may never have been written. The word is the same either way.

Other points:

3. The cancelled word: before `a fénykép` the writer struck out about five signs with a heavy violet stroke, beginning `1. 5,`, with `3.` as the fourth sign (`work/crops/g2.png`, `review/work/z_canc.png`). The third may be `1:` rather than `1.`, which would hint at `a lev...`, but I do not read it. It was cancelled by the writer and is not part of the message.
4. `készevan`: the signs give exactly `7 2. 7. 1: 3. 1. 6,`, and that is what I print. The sense "is ready" rests on `készen van` with the n omitted, so that n is a restoration. All signs are clear.
5. `feviszem`: no l is written, as if `fe(l)viszem`. The rest of the card writes `fel`.
6. `pénszt`, `külgyél`: phonetic spellings (`aszt` on hcp-1172 is the same habit). `gy` in `külgyél` stands for the spoken form of `küldjél`.
7. `zengik el hattyúdalom`: the three dots after it are on the card. `hattyúdalom` lacks an accusative `-at`, as verse may; so the sense is "sing out [my] swan song" with "my swan song" read as the object. The swan-song word and the rhyme with `mondhatom` are secure.
8. `I` and `II` are small numerals above the rows, marking the two stanzas; the `I` stands above the row and could be read as a stray tick, but `II` is plain. This may also explain the `II` marks on hcp-1174, which that card left open.
9. Row order: the violet note is taken as a separate postscript; its place in the reading order is unknown. It uses the same key and the same signs in a different pen.
10. Postmark: not fixed. KÖVES can be read on the ring; the date row is weak and partly under the stamp, and I could not confirm month or day. The date comes from the card's own `1915. XI/11.` and the portal's file name.
11. The verse source was not searched; it may be a printed or popular song. The sign `Sz. A.` is taken as on the sister cards; the bar under it is a flourish.

## Why I believe it is correct

- A key taken from other cards gives continuous Hungarian over 349 of 351 signs, with no forced words. The only family disagreement is `5.` (ly here, l on hcp-1178, 1179 and 1166), stated in the key section.
- The verse scans (8/7 syllables alternating) and rhymes without any help from me: `szirom` / `sírom`, `hattyúdalom` / `mondhatom`.
- The open foot note repeats the content of the cipher: "ha ... rosz üdő lenne és nem mehetnék" continues "ha nagyon rosz üdő lesz nem mehetek". The cipher `számtalanszor csókollak` matches the open formula on hcp-1170 and 1171.
- `Kedves Margitkám` is the family salutation, and the photograph note fits hcp-1170 (December 1915), where the writer deals with the photographer about a picture.
- The whole-card alphabet is regular (comma plain, dot palatal), which was not assumed but came out of the words.
