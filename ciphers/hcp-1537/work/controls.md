# Focused same-card controls

Indices refer to work/indexed-transcription.tsv; only cipher shapes are counted. Shape labels are a transcription convention. These visual comparisons, rather than grammatical expectation, determine the literal base letters.

- Row 11.11–13: `b h 4` → `pri`, in detail-pri.png. Final shape is the open-topped angular 4, not upright 1. Compare row 1.9 (I in DIK), row 3.2 (I in JIZ), and row 1.7 (upright 1=Y). Row 11 is `pri`, not `pry`.
- Row 10.4–11: `b h 4 c d 4 r 3` → `pristiho`, in detail-pristiho.png. Both 4 shapes are visible. No inferred extra letter is inserted.
- Row 11.23–30: `f 6 n p 5 h s 5` → `vamberce`, in detail-row11right.png. Eight glyphs: the fourth is the descending p-shaped B, sixth is tall h-shaped R, seventh is pointed s-shaped C. This is not FAMILCE. Vamberce is retained literally; identifying the named location is a separate historical question.
- Row 13.26–30: `p h q g 1` → `brzky`, in detail-brzy.png (filename retained from the first quick reading). There are FIVE shapes, not four. The q-shaped Z has an open descending stroke, followed by g-shaped K with a closed lower loop. Compare row 1.10 (K in DIK, detail-dik-za.png), row 1.11 (Z in ZA), row 3.3–4 (Z in JIZ/ZE). The full row 13 crop preserves all five.
- Row 12.6: `F` → F in DEFINITIVNE, visibly shallow v-like, distinguished from tall looped `f` → V in the same word (row 12.12). The family shallow-v/F correspondence was known from the published pc199/RUDOLFINA comparison before transcription. It is not an independently discovered letter.
