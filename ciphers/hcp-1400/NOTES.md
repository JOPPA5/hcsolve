Confirmed on the pixels: qmfbtf fydvtf / uxp dbset / pomz ljoe Jib?f = PLEASE EXCUSE / TWO CARDS / ONLY KIND I HA?E (Caesar −1).

New observations (not enough to complete):
- Jib?f: at 6x the sign between b and f shows two downstrokes then the up-stroke into f, i.e. the outline looks more like cipher u (HATE) than w (HAVE, which would need a third arch / top hook as in this writer's v of fydvtf). Context strongly favours HAVE. Resolution of the scan (1024 px) does not settle it; left as a disclosed ambiguity.
- Header: "S.7." + long flourish, as on hcp-1399 ("S.7. Sunday."); treated as uncertain heading, not deciphered.
- Salutation line: first group is a looped capital + z. On 1399 the "Nz" (MY) N has a similar loop, so Nz→MY is plausible but the capital here is shaped differently (L/S-like). Second group E.h. matches 1399's E.h. (→ D.G. if ciphered; meaning unknown). Third group: two parallel slanted strokes joined by an ink blob (k/H/11-like), a long descender (p/j/g-like), a bowl, then n/m with a long horizontal tail; a dot sits above the middle. Candidate decodings (JOL, GOM, KOM, "FROM" from gspn, a time "11 p.m.", ...) all require forcing; none is adopted.
- Closing: "z?s S.h. C?z." The middle sign of z?s is a tall stroke + arch (could be p, giving YOR, or a compressed pv → YOUR, or h → YGR). The capital in S.h. is shaped differently from the header's E (top loop, S-like) → R.G. if ciphered. In C?z the middle sign is a long stroke from above x-height to below the baseline with a small bowl — consistent with this writer's p (as in pomz/uxp), giving BOY, but h (BGY) is not excluded. "Your ... Boy" from a male sender would not sit easily with 1399's "Your own E.h."; not adopted.

Why it stops: the salutation's third word and the closing word/initials cannot be read letter-by-letter from this 1024-px scan without forcing words. A higher-resolution scan of the address side would be needed. No marker created.
