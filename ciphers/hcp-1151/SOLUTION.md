# hcp-1151 solution (after review 1)

Solver: Claude Opus 5.5, 2026-11-02. No earlier work on this number. The transcription is my own, made from `work/hires/`. The portal status is Not solved and it attaches no solution or transcription. The HistoCrypt article the portal links was not checked for this card, so I cannot say whether it discusses it. The key starts from the FINAL sister cards hcp-1174, 1178 and 1179 (Claude Opus 5.5); four signs are new here, and two of them conflict with filed sister readings (see the key section).

## Card

Picture postcard (waterfall view), Mezőkövesd, to Sziber Margitka, Szomolya. Open text: only the place and date `Mezőkövesd, 1916. V/13` and the address `Őnagysága / Sziber Margitka / Kisasszonynak / Szomolya / (u. p. Bogács.) / (Borsod m.)`. All the rest, the whole message side, is digit cipher in red ink. Language: Hungarian (the portal says Unknown). Green printed `Impart` and `1386` are the publisher's, not cipher.

## Cipher type and key

Monoalphabetic substitution with a digit-plus-mark alphabet (register label: MASC). Each letter is one digit with an optional mark. There are no word divisions in the cipher; those in the transcription are mine. Accents o/ó, ö/ő, i/í, ü/ű are not separated by the key.

| sign | letter | sign | letter | sign | letter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0` | o, ó | `3` | d | `6` | j |
| `0,` | ö, ő | `3,` | r | `6,` | n |
| `1` | i, í | `3.` | v | `6.` | ny **(new)** |
| `1.` | a | `4` | f | `7` | k |
| `1:` | e | `4,` | g | `7,` | s |
| `1'` (high dot) | á | `4.` | gy **(new)** | `7.` | sz |
| `1,` | b | `5` | h | `8` | m |
| `:1` | ü, ű | `5,` | l | `8,` | t |
| `2,` | cs | `5.` | ly **(here; l on hcp-1178, 1179)** | `8.` | ty **(here; t? on hcp-1179)** |
| `2.` | é | `9` | p | `9,` | z |

The writer always crosses his 7. `6` = j is not on the tables of hcp-1174 and 1179; `4` = f was only tentative on hcp-1179. On this card a dot on the base of a single letter gives the digraph of that letter (g/gy, l/ly, n/ny, t/ty, also s/sz).

Conflict with filed cards: hcp-1178 and 1179 file `5.` as plain l (`csókollak`, `a legszeb`), and hcp-1179 files `8.` as `t?` (`kar[t?]im`). So the pattern is not family-wide. I keep it for this card because here every ordinary l is written `5,` (30 signs; the one exception is the bare `5` of `Lapodat`) and the two `5.` stand exactly at the two ly positions, melynek and fogoly. `8.` occurs once. This is thin evidence from one card, and the writer may be loose about the mark elsewhere. If `5.` were plain l here, the text would be melnek, fogol: the same words in a different spelling, so the reading does not depend on it. It may bear on the open `kar[t?]im` of hcp-1179 (`kártyám`?), but I change nothing there.

## Plaintext (Hungarian)

Spelling as on the card. Word division and commas are mine. The writer's own marks are the `!` after the heading, the curved brackets around the sentence on conscription, a tall slash between `egészséges` and `Szibériába` (not a sign: taller than a 1, with no flag), the hyphens, and a lone `)` before `Árpád`. All of it is enciphered except the date and address.

Mezőkövesd, 1916. V/13.

Édes Margitkám!

Lapodat megkaptam, melynek igazán megörültem, hisz hogy ne, mikor már egy hónapja hogy nem kaptam tőlled. (Most pedig értesítlek, hogy csak a jövő hónap ötödikén soroznak.) Holnap nem megyek fel, mert most fogom levetetni magamat. Továbbá értesítlek, hogy meghányattam a kártyát, jól beszélt: megmondta aszt is, hogy valaki közülünk fogoly és nagyon jó dolga van, nincs semi baja, pénze van elég és kapt is. Megmondta, hogy nem soká kapok tőle hírt, é igaz is let, mert épen ma kaptam tőle két lapot, egyiket apámnak írja, másikat meg egész családnak, írja hogy egészséges, Szibériába van.

Hegyen ülök, völgybe nézek,
ellátok a messzeségbe,
ott lakik egy szőke lányka,
ez én lelkem üdvössége.

Sokszor ölel és csókol szerető hű kedvesed Árpád

Normal forms of the writer's spellings: tőlled = tőled, aszt = azt, semi = semmi, kapt = kap, é = és, let = lett, Szibériába van = Szibériában van.

## English translation

Sweet Margitka! I received your card and was truly glad of it, and why would I not be, when it has been a month now since I had one from you. (And now I inform you that they will conscript only on the fifth of next month.) Tomorrow I am not going up, because I am about to have my photograph taken. Further, I inform you that I had the cards thrown for me, and they spoke well: they also told that one of us is a prisoner and is doing very well, nothing is wrong with him, he has enough money and also gets some. They told that I would soon have news from him, and that came true too, for just today I got two cards from him, one written to my father, the other to the whole family; he writes that he is healthy and is in Siberia.

I sit on a hill, I look into the valley, / I see far into the distance; / there lives a blonde girl, / this is the salvation of my soul.

Many times your loving, faithful sweetheart Árpád embraces and kisses you.

## Command

Run from the repo root:

    python3 -I ciphers/hcp-1151/work/decode.py

It checks the SHA-256 of all four scans, decodes every sign of `work/transcription.txt` with the table, stops on any disagreement with the stated reading, and prints the sign counts and the full text. The accents and the reading text are in the script, so the check is that the signs decode to those letters.

## Uncertainties

Result: 574 signs, 120 words; 569 signs decode by the table as read, 5 are read by word sense (`~` in the transcription). No word or clause is unread.

Signs by word sense:

1. `Lapodat`: first sign is a plain `5` = h, no comma found; the key gives `hapodat`. Sense needs l. It is the only l of the card without a comma.
2. `továbbá`: the sixth sign is retouched in darker ink and shaped between `1,` and `5,`. I take it as `1,` (b). `5,` would give `továbla`. In the same word the mark after the 3 is a short comma with a head, so the sign is `3,` in shape and the table gives r (`torábbá`); I read v by sense, written `3,~v` in the transcription. A small extra dot stands beside the comma of the opening `8,`; it changes nothing. This word is the weakest point, though the context ("Further, I inform you") fits.
3. `ott` (two signs): the t's are heavy with ink and their digits are not cleanly legible; 8-like with commas. The sense comes from the line. Earlier I saw them as 3-like, which gives `ordd`, not a word.

`szőke` is no longer in this list: its first sign is `7.` with the dot run into the end of the crossbar, as read.

Other points:

- `ez én lelkem`: the card has no word division. `ezén` would need a wrong long vowel; `ez én` needs no correction and keeps eight syllables.
- `é` in `é igaz is let`: the sign is clearly `2.`, with no s after it. I take it as a slip for `és`.
- `kapt is`: `1 7,` opens the row under printed `Impart`, with the 1 and 7 pale but readable. `kapt` for `kap` is my reading of the writer's form; the sentence could mean he "gets" or "got" money. The card has a writer's hyphen after the t at the end of row 11, beyond the green line, so literally `kapt-` / `is`. Hyphens elsewhere split a word (`meg-örültem`, `maga-mat`, `Szibériá-ba`), so either he wrote `kaptis` as one unit or the hyphen is loose. The letters are the same either way. The transcription has `-`, not `=`, because I keep the word break.
- `baja`: the first sign is partly under the printed `Impart`; read as `1,`. `6,` would give `naja`, not a word.
- `:1` in `közülünk`, `hű`: the left dots are partly merged with the stem, sometimes one dot is clear. Word sense separates ü from ű; both are the same sign.
- Under the `1:` of `levetetni` there are two small unexplained strokes. They do not change the sign.
- Word order in the lower block: the dashed frame around the right part separates the signature from the poem, so I read the frame last. Reading across instead gives `nézek sokszor`, which breaks nothing in the sense but spoils the verse; the letters are the same.
- A pale 7-shaped stroke after `Árpád` is uncrossed, unlike every 7 of the writer, and joins the dashed frame. I take it as a flourish, not a sign.
- `meghányattam a kártyát` as "had the cards thrown": the words are clear, the fortune-telling sense comes from context and is my interpretation.
- `soroznak` as conscription: the word fits 1916 and the writer's situation, not otherwise confirmed.

## Why I believe it is correct

- The key comes from other cards, and with only four added digraph signs it turns 574 signs into continuous Hungarian with no gaps.
- One pattern on this card covers the new signs, though it is not family-wide (see the key section), and three of them recur: `4.` in hogy, egy, völgybe; `5.` in melynek, fogoly; `6.` in meghányattam, lányka. `8.` occurs once, in kártyát.
- The content fits the place and date: 1916, conscription, a family member held prisoner in Siberia.
- The poem has four lines of eight or so syllables with the rhyme messzeségbe / üdvössége.
- The signature `Árpád` and the heading `Édes Margitkám` match the filed reading of hcp-1174, and the address is the same Margitka Sziber. hcp-1179 is signed Sz. A. in clear and only quotes the hcp-1174 heading, so it is no independent support for these words.
