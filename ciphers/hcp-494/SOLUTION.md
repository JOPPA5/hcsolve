### Terminal pair: XX provisionally favored by shape

The original has **two separate crossed forms**, like XX in passage 1. The added inverse-key control explicitly gives **XX=u** and includes small loops on the upper arms; therefore the little loop on the terminal pair is not evidence distinguishing ff. In contrast, the key-255 **ff symbol has two staves joined by diagonal strokes**. Comparable connections are not visible in the target.

As a check on the suggestion that the writer simply omitted connectors, the original **st** and **tt** symbols retain their upper and lower connecting curves. This weakens a blanket omission explanation but cannot establish how the writer drew ff: there is **no independent same-writer encrypted ff control**. Ordinary clear-text ff would not be a direct control for this special cipher symbol. The inspected key254 sheet confirms XX=u; it does not independently supply the ff symbol.

**28 October qualification:** the ff graphic can also be parsed as two crossed components whose diagonal arms overlap the neighbouring stave. Wider spacing could remove their contact without deleting an independent connector. The st/tt comparison does not test this specific spacing hypothesis. Thus the earlier connector argument was too strong if used to deny any shape-based mechanism for ff. The hypothesis is not a demonstrated writing convention, and the same visible target remains directly compatible with XX=u. No new third glyph or supported segmentation was found. Keys 253 and 266 use different ff graphics and do not corroborate this proposed variant. See `work/ending-alternative-2026-10-28.md` and its unretouched comparison board. The provisional XX preference and both decoding branches remain unchanged; selection of the fluent ff branch still relies on language.

Consequently **XX=u is the provisional shape preference; ff remains a language-driven candidate**. A simplified ff could have become visually indistinguishable from XX, allowing u in passage 1 and ff in passage 2. That possible graphic collision is not established here. Nor is an enciphering slip or a changed key cell established. Globally replacing XX=u with ff would give `disgffstirt` in passage 1; this rejects neither every local collision model nor every possible scribal error. No global replacement is applied.

## Number-only test of nomenclator renumbering

The 26 large-number occurrences comprise **17 distinct codes** with the preferred 427 reading, or **16** if that occurrence remains 447. The complete word-list envelope of key 255 is **180–407**. A new exhaustive test admits every integer in that interval, even gaps, and asks whether one fixed arithmetic transformation and digit permutation can place all target codes inside it. **No annotation-derived meaning is used in this primary test.**

For each transcription, `work/audit_nomenclator_numbering.py` checks all 6 digit orders, 400 invertible multipliers modulo 1000, and 1000 offsets, with digit permutation before or after the arithmetic: **4,800,000 parameter choices** per transcription, with overlap between the two families. None fits all codes. The best numerical range coverage is 13 of 17 or 13 of 16 respectively; this is not plaintext accuracy or an accepted key. Even discarding one distinct code would not rescue these families. Direct-count checks and two constructed positive controls validate the implementation, not the historical model.

This excludes this particular fixed renumbering mechanism under the stated transcription and old-word-list assumptions. It does not exclude a new or expanded nomenclator, different rules for separate blocks, arbitrary substitutions, or other mechanisms. A separate, explicitly annotation-dependent test also rejects a common offset and order-preserving list expansion for five provisional word correspondences. Saved results and all limits: `work/nomenclator-numbering-audit.json`. No new word-code value is accepted.

## Independent panel, language and context, 29 October 2026 (Claude contribution)

Written by Claude Fable 5.1 at the human's request; Claude is a contributor here, not the independent reviewer. Details, scripts, crops and sources: `work/claude-2026-10-29/` and the matching `NOTES.md` section.

**Glyphs.** Nine subagents (three lenses per sign) judged the three disputed signs from the original scans and both key tables, unanimously: **PP = n** (0.80–0.85), **II = g** (0.75–0.85), **terminal XX = u as drawn** (0.75–0.80). The solver's main contrary control for PP, "b/B in breviat", is the capital P of *Prædicat* and supports PP; "Portrait" is also a single-bow P; the key's ff is a joined monogram whose features the terminal pair lacks, and the 28 October spacing hypothesis misdescribes it. Key 254's header names *Secretarium Linckern*.

**Language.** `undertan | in` is the only segmentation of the ten letters that is German in sense (requires n); bare plural *Unterthan* is unattested, the collective singular *der Unterthan* is, so `[der] Undertan` or `Undertan[en]` are both restorations. *ablauffe* / *wol ablauffen* are well attested 1628–1702, the cipher's own `geb` is apocopated, and **`ablauu` is nothing in period German**; *all* for *alles* is attested in Magdeburg 1672. Reading: the sign is XX, the intended text is `ablauff`.

**Context.** The Rendsburg Landtag sat 24–29 April 1672; the dispute was 12 vs 10 Reichstaler per Pflug, with threats of execution — matching the clear text. Duke Hans Adolf of Plön, imperial Feldmarschallleutnant, carried the Emperor's alliance draft to Copenhagen in Feb 1672 and was at Rendsburg on 27 April; the Danish–Brunswick alliance was signed 31 March 1672, and the alliance still under negotiation on 4 May was the bilateral **Denmark–Emperor** treaty. So the gloss **Kayser** at 447 602 fits and "Haus Braunschweig" does not; 447 602 is best read as one two-part designation of the Emperor. Blumenthal is Christoph Caspar v. B. (dismissal letter 26 April 1672); Ahlefeldt was Statthalter from 1663.

**Prior work to credit.** Cipher-Atelier (Maxim Egorov), https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/lincker-to-vultejus-cipher-letter-1672, published 6 Oct 2026, has a correction dated 2 Oct 2026 reading group 17 as PP = n by the same Proposition comparison, output `disgustirtundertanin[634]`, and sequence B `{g}ottgebdasallw{d/o}lablau{u/ff}`; no code values.
