## Passage 1: key output and secondary margin reconstruction

Literal application of the comparison letter table, with the country supplied by its gloss:

```text
disgustirtundertazin<Holstein: gloss>
```

Without the country annotation, the strictly key-based output is `disgustirtundertazin<634>`. Direct comparison still favors BB=z over PP=n. Numeric 66=d is a weaker historical transcription alternative, giving `disgustirtundertadin<634>`; neither alternative yields a secure contextual reading. No original-sign basis for replacing this token with n has been established.

Claude reads the disputed margin letter as **n**, rather than r or z. Its low form provides a palaeographic reason for the working segmentation `disgustirt | undertan | in | Holstein`. This supersedes our earlier assessment that `undertan in` had no evidence beyond linguistic speculation. Evidence: `work/margin-gloss.jpg` and `work/margin-gloss-erta-n.jpg`.

A further same-page comparison on 25 October puts the disputed pair beside numeric **6** in **67, 26, 63, 96 and 625** (`work/numeral-six-controls.png`). The 96 control is partly overlapped by a gloss. The disputed pair's small lower loops and elongated upper strokes remain compatible with the preferred BB reading. Numeric handwriting varies, so this is not a definitive BB/66 classifier; no original-sign evidence for PP=n was obtained. The sheet preserves black annotations and native crop pixels.

A contextual reconstruction is:

> Totaliter disgustirt [sind die] Undertan[en] in Holstein, nun so wohl als in Dennemarck Adell, Bürger und Bauren … in plainten combiniret.

Approximately: “The subjects in Holstein are completely displeased; there, as in Denmark, nobles, burghers and peasants … have joined in complaints.” This combines clear text, gloss readings and editorial supplies; it is not an uninterrupted deciphered sentence.

**The cipher glyph remains BB, which gives z in both comparison tables; n would be PP.** Adopting the margin reading therefore requires a local BB→n restoration, not a corrected transcription of BB as PP. An enciphering/copying slip, a different 1672 key cell, and an annotator's interpretation remain distinct possibilities.

`[sind die]` and `[en]` are editorial supplies, not recovered characters. The period corpus contains plural `unterthanen`/`underthanen`, but does not establish that the source's apparent `undertan` is a shortened plural. The grammatical restoration and margin-letter identification remain probable interpretations, not proof of the intended plaintext. The former segmentation `und | ERTAZ | in` was itself provisional.

The passage-2 annotation **amm** conflicts with our preferred key/shape reading **all**, as Claude subsequently pointed out and as Bourdeau had already recorded. This makes reliance on the annotator's proposed n less secure: the annotation cannot be treated as ground truth overriding BB=z. It does not by itself refute the n reading or prove which side of the amm/all discrepancy is wrong.

## Passage 2: a candidate using the unchanged key

Literal comparison output using the preserved source placeholders:

```text
<Sueco: gloss>hottgebdasa?wolabl∅u
```

Bourdeau's working interpretation is:

> [Sueco:] Gott geb das all wol ablauff.

“[Sweden:] May God grant that everything turns out well.”

There is now a way to obtain its letter sequence directly from the comparison key, **if the initial hooked pair is II rather than numeric 55**, and the last two ambiguous signs are au and ff:

```text
II 76 [tt] 63 38 22 FF 20 75 20 [ll] YY 96 NN 50 32 110 [au] [ff]
g  o  tt   g  e  b  d  a  s  a  ll   w  o  l  a  b  l   au   ff
```

In both the forward table 255 and inverse table 254, the doubled I/J-shaped character represents **g**. Numeric **55 remains h**. Compared with the writer's 5 in 75, 85 and 625, the disputed pair has two elongated hooked strokes that can be read as I/J forms. The key's crossbars are not securely visible in the target, so numeric 55 is not excluded. See `work/glyph-first-initial-comparison.png`. This retracts the earlier assertion that the shape is definitively 55, without replacing it with a definitive II claim.

A further comparison with numeric 55 in the auxiliary 1677 letter (`work/lincker1677-55.png`) shows compact numeral heads, but the five-year gap and unestablished identity of the writing hand prevent using it to decide the 1672 pair. Reinspection also did not establish a distinguishing connecting stroke in the final ff/XX-like pair. Neither ambiguity is newly resolved, and no hidden brown stroke beneath a black annotation has been reconstructed.

The strict command retains code 768 as `<768>`; Sueco/Sweden is a separate annotation-derived interpretation, not decoded by this comparison word list.

The comparison sheet `work/syllable-evidence-sheet.png` includes the target signs, the key forms, and the mm/XX controls. It is made from ordinary unretouched crops by `work/current_evidence_crops.py`.

**The amm/all discrepancy is explicit evidence against treating every annotation as authoritative.** Confusion between the adjacent similar ll/mm signs is a plausible explanation. It does not establish that the annotator possessed this key or that the annotator, rather than our transferred table or glyph interpretation, is wrong. Retain all as the preferred working reading on shape and linguistic grounds, with amm preserved as the conflicting annotation. Claude identified this omission in feedback supplied by the user; it was not a new discovery of the annotation or a completed independent review.

## Nomenclator and the alliance clause

Most contextual code readings remain borrowed from the manuscript glosses through Bourdeau. The preferred visual correction **427 rather than 447 before 641 / Plön** separates that occurrence from the later 447, invalidating the argument that a repeated 447 proves Herzog at both positions. See `work/427-vs-447.png`; this correction still awaits independent palaeographic review.

The ending is best recorded at phrase level:

> eine estroite <303 alliance> zwischen <447 602: Kayser, phrase gloss; internal allocation unresolved> und <834 Rex Daniae: gloss> unter Handen

Approximately: “a close alliance under negotiation between the Emperor [per the corrected gloss] and the King of Denmark.” The surviving Kayser annotation may explain **the whole pair 447 602**. Its position does not establish a standalone 602=Kayser entry. Neither does 447 establish an additional person; the editorial comma formerly inserted between 447 and 602 is withdrawn. Do not assign 447 to Herzog, Haus, dem or a null without further evidence. The earlier Haus Braunschweig interpretation comes from a crossed/overwritten gloss and is not adopted.

The phrase `unter Handen` supports ongoing negotiation, not a concluded alliance. An official Prussian archive catalogue records a later defensive alliance of 12/22 September 1672 involving Denmark, the Emperor and other parties; this is historical context already discussed in the published attempt, **not evidence for a particular value of the May letter's 447 or 602**. Source and acquisition details are in `SOURCES.md`.

Code 625 and its title/name annotation remain uncertain. Reinspection suggests a possible chancellor title, but does not justify replacing the prior uncertain reading or identifying the person more precisely.
