# Lincker and the Hessian cipher keys

Research status as of 5 October 2026: **a surviving key has been applied in part; the letter is not fully deciphered.**

## Research task

Check whether a key associated with Secretary Lincker in 1666 explains the cipher in his letter to Chancellor Vultejus from Hamburg in May 1672, and separate direct key readings from uncertain historical glosses.

The letter is HStAM 4 f Staaten D, Dänemark 125, indexed as [HCPortal cryptogram 494](https://crypto.hcportal.eu/dashboard/cryptograms/494). The manuscript date reads 4 May 1672, with a separate 4/14 May endorsement. The portal's 3 May date conflicts with that source evidence and is not silently substituted for it.

## Sources examined

The audit examined all six images in the letter file and all 16 images of HStAM 4 d Nr. 1234. Images 13–16 of the key volume contain the relevant alphabet, syllabic material, vocabulary and an inscription associating its use with Lincker in 1666. The inverse table on image 12 provides additional comparison material.

The letter already contains historical marginal and interlinear readings. Recovering their content through the surviving key is therefore not a first decipherment of wholly unannotated text.

## Tests and results

Direct application of the alphabet and syllabic signs supports the opening *disgustirt und…*. Image comparison corrected several numeral-like transcriptions into genuine syllabic signs.

An important later correction concerns a doubled letter-like group. It was first labelled BB and read as z. Comparison with the same writer's clear word *Proposition* favored PP; the key gives PP = n. The corrected literal main-block output is:

`disgustirtundertanin[634]`

The spacing *disgustirt undertan in [634]* is an editorial possibility. Its historical syntax and the meaning of 634 are unresolved. A difficult marginal word may read *Holstein*, but the audit did not recover a dictionary entry establishing 634 = Holstein.

The lower passage supports a cautious candidate resembling *gott geb das all wol ablau[f(f)?]*. Overwritten digits and the final sign remain uncertain; the phrase is not promoted to an exact complete reading.

A search for supplementary keys covered the available key volume, the 21 indexed HStAM 4 d cipher entries, relevant name variants and the 1666–1676 period. The adjacent key volume Nr. 1235, dated approximately 1670, did not match the inspected alphabet and code ranges. This was a bounded archival search, not proof that no matching supplement survives.

## Why the reconstruction stops

The available 1666 vocabulary ends at 407, while the letter uses codes in the 600–800 range. Alphabet agreement cannot identify those absent dictionary entries. Both the main unresolved code and several other word codes therefore require a matching supplement, another securely related key or an independent full historical reading.

The useful result is a corrected partial application of a surviving key, with uncertainty preserved. There is no verified full plaintext and no claim of a newly discovered cipher system.
