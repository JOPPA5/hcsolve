# HCP 494 – historical context for the alliance clause (Lincker to Vultejus, Hamburg 4 May 1672)

Helper note by Claude (Fable 5.1), 29 Oct 2026. Context only; it recovers no key entry. Everything below
is from published literature/catalogues reached via WebFetch; OCR texts (runeberg, archive.org) were
read in summary or grep form, so page numbers are reliable but exact wording should be re-checked
against the scans before being quoted in a report.

## 0. The letter's own frame (from source/arcinsys/metadata.md and the solver's reading)

- Arcinsys title of HStAM 4 f Staaten D, Dänemark 125: "Sekretär Linker schreibt von Hamburg über seine
  Behandlung am dänischen Hofe u. die des brandenburgischen Gesandten, klagt über Brandenburgs
  Prätensionen in Etikettefragen; auch über auswärtige Politik (teilweise in Chiffren) u.
  Landtagsproposition zu Rendsburg" (1672). So Lincker had just been at the Danish court/Landtag and
  wrote on the way back, from Hamburg.
- Clear text mentions: 630 Blumenthal and his "abfertigung" (dismissal), "Foedus Dano-Brunsvicense",
  625 (gloss: Statthalter Ahlefeldt?) going to Berlin so that 651 Brandenburg "in das Foedus
  Dano-Brunsvicense mit eingezogen werde", France concluded with Sweden and passed Denmark over,
  the Landstände "protestando voneinander geschieden ... ad rigorem Executionis", then
  "<427> <641 Plön> war auch zu Hamburg, und hat selbiger eine estroite <303 alliance> zwischen
  <447> <602> und <834 Rey Danae> unter handlung, und wird selbige ehistes tages zum stande kommen."

Other brothers: August (1635-99, Norburg), Joachim Ernst II (1637-1700, Rethwisch), Bernhard (1639-76,
Danish general). None is styled "Herzog von Plön" after 1671 and none is recorded as negotiating an
alliance in 1672. Joachim Ernst (the father) is excluded by his death on 5 Oct 1671.

**Hamburg itself**: I found no source placing Hans Adolf in Hamburg in April/May 1672; the letter is the
only attestation seen. It is fully consistent: Hamburg lies on his route Berlin -> Rendsburg (arrival
27 Apr), Plön is 50 km from Hamburg, and the 1670-71 Danish-Plön commission talks had sat in Hamburg
(DNT VI p. 456, https://runeberg.org/danotrak/6/0468.html ).

**Whom was he negotiating for?** The Emperor's side (he was an imperial Feldmarschallleutnant carrying
the imperial draft to Copenhagen), but also in his own interest (Oldenburg judgment) and openly as a
promoter of the anti-French combination (Emperor, Brandenburg, Brunswick, Hesse).

Reading of the letter against this:
- "Foedus Dano-Brunsvicense" (clear text) = the Braunschweig treaty of 31 Mar 1672, already concluded
  five weeks before the letter. The letter's "dass Churbrandenburg in das Foedus Dano-Brunsvicense mit
  eingezogen werde" is exactly the extension that produced the 12 Sep 1672 alliance.
- The alliance that was still "unter handlung" and expected "ehistes tages" on 4 May 1672 can
  therefore not be the Danish-Brunswick one. The only bilateral alliance of the King of Denmark under
  negotiation at that moment, and the one Hans Adolf of Plön personally carried between Vienna,
  Berlin and Rendsburg, is the **Denmark-Emperor defensive alliance** (Danish draft 13 Feb 1672).
  Lincker's optimism ("ehistes tages") was wrong: it stalled in 1672 and was signed only 16 Jan 1674.
- Plön's 19 Mar 1672 letter ("kejseren, Brunsvig og Hessen er med") shows that in Hamburg/Rendsburg
  he was also advertising the wider combination; Jørgensen II p. 29 adds "Østrig og Spanien var rede
  til at gå med [Holland og Brandenburg] og ventede kun på Danmark".

## 3. Implication for 447 602 (context, not key recovery)

- The uncancelled gloss **"Kayser"** fits the history; the struck "Haus Braunschweig" does not (that
  alliance was already signed and is named in clear as Foedus Dano-Brunsvicense). Brandenburg is
  handled separately in the same letter (625 to go to Berlin), and the States General were only being
  approached (Werkendam arrived 6 May).
- "zwischen <447 602> und <834 Rex Daniae>" names two treaty parties. Historically the second party is
  the Emperor alone. So the likeliest reading is that 447 602 together render the Emperor, i.e. one
  two-part designation (e.g. "Röm. Kays." + "Maj.", "Ihre" + "Kays. Maj.", "Kayser" + a null/filler)
  rather than two different princes. A two-entry reading "Kayser + Spanien" is conceivable (Spain was
  expected to join, Jørgensen II p. 29; Spanish subsidies appear in the 1674 secret articles) but has
  no support in the gloss and the Feb 1672 draft was strictly bilateral. "Kayser und Reich" is a
  stock phrase but the draft was with the Emperor as Austrian/Imperial sovereign, not the Reich.
  Nothing here can settle how the phrase splits across the two numbers.
- 427/447 before 641 "Plön": the person is Hans Adolf; whether 427 (or 447) is "Herzog" / "Herzog von"
  cannot be decided historically, but note that the same letter writer would need "Herzog von" + "Plön"
  - a title + name pair - which parallels a title + name split for the Emperor.

## 4. 834 "Rey Danae" vs 601 "König in Dennemarck / Dennemarck"

- Both are plausible as distinct codebook entries: nomenclators of this family regularly carry a
  country and its sovereign separately (the related list in HCP 266 has "Dänemark 185"; the glossator
  himself renders 601 both as "K. Dennemarck"/"der König in Dennemarck" and as plain "Dennemarck").
- Historically the treaty party in the alliance clause is the sovereign ("Rex Daniae", formal treaty
  style), while the other five 601 occurrences are narrative ("in Dänemark", "the King ... respect",
  "Denmark passed over"). A split 601 = Dänemark / 834 = König in Dänemark (or "Ihre Kön. Maj. zu
  Dänemark") would be natural; the reverse cannot be excluded. The gloss "Rey Danae" is an abbreviated
  "Rex Daniae". Not decidable from history.

## 5. Side observations for the solver

- Passage 1 context: the Rendsburg Landtag of April 1672 (King, Griffenfeld, Ahlefeldt, Gyldenløve,
  Blumenthal, Plön all present; the estates parted "protestando"). "undertan in Holstein ... Adel,
  Bürger und Bauern" fits a reading "Unterthan[en] in Holstein"; note the cipher gives no "-en".
- Ahlefeldt's planned journey to Berlin (625): not verified; what the sources show is that Brandenburg
  sent Blumenthal to Copenhagen again (late June) and the joint negotiation moved to Braunschweig
  (Aug-Sep 1672).
- A further third-party partial reading of HCP 494 exists on GitHub (Cipher-Atelier /
  lincker-to-vultejus-cipher-letter-1672, status 11 Oct 2026; reads sequence A as
  "disgustirtundertanin[634]" with PP=n, and sequence B as "{g}ottgebdasallw{d/o}lablau{u/ff}").
  Not found in SOURCES.md; the solver should record it: https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/lincker-to-vultejus-cipher-letter-1672
- "Ribbeck 1899" could not be identified (searches return only the classicist Otto Ribbeck); the
  Urkunden und Actenstücke series has no obvious Denmark volume for 1672 in what I could reach.
- Hesse-Kassel: Hedwig Sophie kept neutrality until 1673 per de.wikipedia, but was party to the Seesen
  pact (27 Apr 1672) and the Braunschweig alliance (12 Sep 1672); the Marburg marriage-contract
  edition lists "Defensivallianz 12. September 1672 zusammen mit Kaiser, Brandenburg, BrL-Celle,
  BrL-Wolfenbüttel" (https://dynastische-ehevertraege.online.uni-marburg.de/de/vertraege/10.pdf).

## 6. Not verified / limits

- No primary text placing Plön in Hamburg in spring 1672 (only the letter).
- DNT and Jørgensen were read from OCR; quotations should be checked on the scans.
- The imperial side's negotiator in Copenhagen in Jan-Feb 1672 is Plön himself (DNT VI p. 525); I found
  no separate imperial envoy in Denmark/Hamburg in spring 1672 (Windischgrätz's Danish mission is
  1673-74; the Dresden list naming Mansfeld 1672 concerns the Saxon court).
