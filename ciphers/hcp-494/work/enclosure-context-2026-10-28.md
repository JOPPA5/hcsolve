# Original enclosure context — investigation begun 28 October 2026

**New archive lead, not a new cipher solution.** The clear-text continuation independently points to an Anhalt secretary whose surname is best read **Raumer**. The mention of enclosure B provides a specific archival search target. Neither enclosure has been found in this check.

## Reading before external lookup

Source: `source/full/0004.jpg`, original brown writing after the terminal crossed pair. The first reading was sent to the parent agent before any person search:

> Was der Anhaltische / Secretarius Raumer an mich begehret gibt die beylage / Lit. B. und wird [abbreviation unresolved] Cantzler [schon wißen?] [...] / [...] in seinem(?) desiderio zu [...].

`Anhaltische`, `Secretarius`, the probable surname `Raumer`, `an mich begehret`, and `beylage Lit. B.` form the useful span. The capital of the surname has a long descending leg. Raumer is preferred, but the image alone supplies neither a given name nor an authenticated identity. My initial tentative surname guess `Schwei[t]zer` after `Cantzler` is withdrawn: direct rereading makes the ordinary words `schon wißen` more plausible. This passage must not create an invented second official called Schweitzer.

Earlier on the same page, the working reading is:

> Was die Landtags / Proposition zu Rendsburg gewesen gibt die / beylage Lit. A. [...]

The following discussion of a requested contribution and the Landstände supplies political context; its full transcription was outside this microcheck. The labels A and B refer to different enclosures. The manuscript does not establish that B contained cipher material, a key, or a duplicate of either encrypted passage.

## Bounded public lookup

Friedrich Gottlieb Raumer (1643–1728) is a **plausible identity**, not a name supplied by this letter. The scholarly NDB genealogy identifies him as an Anhalt-Dessau privy councillor and places his ennoblement in 1693. [Neue Deutsche Biographie, Raumer family genealogy](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd119059622.html#ndbcontent).

An older lexicon's indexed text says Friedrich Gottlieb served Johann Georg II as secret secretary from 1669 and became Hofrat in 1672. This matches the date and title provisionally, but the 50 MB PDF could not be opened by the web parser and the printed passage was **not visually checked**. This is an explicitly limited auxiliary lead, not the basis of the original reading. [Neu-vermehrtes historisch- und geographisches allgemeines Lexicon, indexed Raumer entry](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/29677417.pdf).

The official LASA catalogue supplies a concrete, digitized correspondence file:

**Landesarchiv Sachsen-Anhalt, Z 44, A 9a IIa Nr. 62; Filmsignatur 5914**, Raumer-family letters and reports to Johann Georg II. Overall dates are **1665–1692**. Its detailed title names Friedrich **Gottfried** von Raumer and gives **1673–1786**; these are the catalogue's actual spelling/date strings and have not been silently corrected. Therefore this file is not yet proof of a letter from the relevant Raumer in May 1672. Adjacent Nr. 61 also names a secretary **Ramus**, who must not be conflated with Raumer. [Exact LASA description](https://recherche.landesarchiv.sachsen-anhalt.de/Query/detail.aspx?ID=502883).

The digitization manifest was retrieved from the catalogue's public viewer. It lists **111 scan pages** in DEFAULT, MIN, MAX and THUMBS variants, plus DOWNLOAD entries. The 556 file entries are not 556 independent pages. No scan from this new file was inspected during the context-identification check. [Public manifest](https://recherche.landesarchiv.sachsen-anhalt.de/xslt/index.aspx?xml=Z_44__A_9a_IIa_Nr_62-producer.xml).

Saved evidence: `lasa-plan.html`, `lasa-raumer-502883.html`, `lasa-raumer-viewer.html`, and `lasa-raumer-mets.xml` in the crop directory. The exact leaf ID was matched from the archive plan's own labels and node metadata; all 133 labels matched 133 nodes, with the selected neighbouring Nr. 66 also checked. For A, the official published finding aid independently lists a Rendsburg Landtag file for 1672: Landesarchiv Schleswig-Holstein, Abt. 65.1 Nr. 311. This is a contextual control and possible parallel-copy lead, not the located enclosure A. [Finding aid, printed page 24](https://d-nb.info/1188022326/34).

## What can be done with the lead

Screen the 111 Raumer scans for dates around 1669–1673, Hamburg, Lincker, Vultejus, and letters that could be enclosure B. The strongest initial place to look remains material physically adjacent to the May 1672 Lincker letter in Dänemark 125; a separate Dessau file could at best yield a draft or related correspondence until demonstrated otherwise. A request by Raumer could concern an ordinary personal or administrative matter, so it must not supply guessed cipher values.
