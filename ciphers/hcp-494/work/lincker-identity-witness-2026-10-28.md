# HCP 494: Lincker identity and sibling-witness search, 2026-10-28

## Bounded result

The Hamburg secretary in HCP 494 is now **strongly identified as Georg Lincker**, through an explicitly named Hessian diplomatic career that leads from Berlin to Danish service and Hamburg. The recipient is **strongly identified as Johannes Vultejus (1605–1684)** by his Kassel chancellorship. These are contextual identifications, not newly read signatures on the target. No new original cipher letter or matching nomenclator was recovered in this search. The identity evidence therefore improves the search target, not the plaintext.

The most useful new source is Walther Ribbeck's 1899 study based on Lincker's reports. It explicitly describes interlinear decipherments in the 1666–1669 correspondence. This is evidence that such annotations existed in related letters by 1899. It **does not date the annotations on HCP 494**, which is a different letter from 1672, and does not establish their accuracy or contemporaneity.

All new downloads and renders are in `work/lincker-identity-witness-2026-10-28/`. Existing source files, SOLUTION.md, NOTES.md, and state markers were not changed by this helper.

The rest of the article was searched/read through its OCR for report references. The footnotes mainly cite individual dates, not archive shelfmarks. It is a thematic account with quotations, not a facsimile edition or a complete diplomatic transcription. No manuscript image is present in the article.

### Corroborating modern scholarship

Thomas Throckmorton, *Das Bekenntnis des Hofmanns: Lutheraner und Reformierte am Hof Friedrich Wilhelms, des Großen Kurfürsten* (De Gruyter Oldenbourg, 2019), source discussion and note 75, independently names the Hessian envoy **Georg Lincker**, cites Ribbeck, and says the originals contain further useful details. ## 2. Official Hessian records provide a compatible chain

- [HStAM 4 f Preußen 345, 1668](https://arcinsys.hessen.de/arcinsys/showArchivalDescriptionDetails.action?archivalDescriptionId=4046380) explicitly calls him **“hessischen Sekretärs Georg Lincker”** and describes his reports to Hedwig Sophie from Berlin/Königsberg and his mission to Denmark. Current representations inspected: Original and Mikrofiche; no online image link observed on that record. Saved `arcinsys-georg1668.html`. This record was already known to the parent; its evidential function is corroboration, not discovery.
- [Target HStAM 4 f Dänemark 125, 1672](https://arcinsys.hessen.de/arcinsys/showArchivalDescriptionDetails.action?archivalDescriptionId=3352629) calls the writer **Sekretär Linker**, writing from Hamburg about his treatment at the Danish court, Brandenburg etiquette claims, foreign policy partly in cipher, and the Rendsburg Landtag proposition. Provenance: **Kanzler Vultejus, Paket 5**. Saved `arcinsys-target.html`. This record does not expand the writer's given name.

Combined with Ribbeck's explicit Hessian/Danish career, these records support identifying the target's secretary with Georg. They do not constitute an independently read target signature. No birth/death dates are assigned to Georg here: secondary sources conflict, and attaching a family genealogy would add an unnecessary identification risk. **Johan Henrik Lincker remains a separate name in the Danish inventories**, not a demonstrated expansion of the target's surname.

## 3. The Chancellor is Johannes Vultejus

The [LWL Internet-Portal Westfälische Geschichte, person 17162](https://www.lwl.org/westfaelische-geschichte/portal/Internet/drucken.php?ID=17162&tab=per) identifies **Johannes Vultejus**, born 6 January 1605 at Marburg, died 16 August 1684 at Kassel. Its career lists Kassel chancellor from **1651–(1684?)** and guardianship councillor 1651–84. The cited source is Lupold von Lehsten, *Die hessischen Reichstagsgesandten im 17. und 18. Jahrhundert*, volume 2, p. 95. Saved `lwl-vultejus.html`.

This office chronology matches the target's “Kanzler Vultejus” in 1672. The target's own address/docket has not newly supplied a given name, so retain the explicit distinction between the manuscript surname and the externally identified person. The question mark attached to 1684 in the institutional register is preserved here.

## What would change the cipher claim

A useful next witness would be an original letter bearing Georg's signature and matching cipher signs, preferably from 1666–72, or a letter with both code numbers and independently verified plaintext. The two career/identity chains alone cannot assign 634, 768, 447, 602 or any other numerical code. Likewise Ribbeck's statement about interlinear solutions cannot turn HCP 494's black notes into the cipher key.
