# Method findings and calibration

Callimahos, Military Cryptanalytics Part III, printed pp. 184–201 (1977), describes several distinct attacks:

* pp. 187–191: original fixed 17-step model. Distribute ciphertext into 26 displacement classes for each selector phase; a correctly aligned grille yields approximately language IC within each class. This requires the known stepping sequence and enough observations in each class. It does not solve an unknown later 52-hole mask. pp. 195–196 explicitly warns that wrong phases have biased displacement mixtures, with expected IC around 1.14 rather than 1.00, and short samples can favor a wrong phase.
* pp. 191–194: transpose those distributions into 26 cipher-letter rows, circularly match rows by mutual coincidence and progressively combine aligned rows. This reconstructs the cipher ring statistically without first knowing the plaintext ring, then reduces the message to MASC. The manual's matching uses an existing displacement partition; it is not a blind unknown-mask search. Our previous IC projection search resembled its marginal objective but skipped this essential known-partition premise.
* pp. 198–199: causal isomorphs at intervals divisible by the active-stop count yield indirect-symmetry chains of the output ring. Same prepared plaintext traversing the same stop phase in another cycle has a constant ring shift. This is ciphertext-only in the usual cryptanalytic sense but assumes correctly recognized repeated plaintext/stepping alignment. Ordinary one-press messages preserve character-index cycle alignment; selective J interruption instead requires equal lever-press progression.
* pp. 200–201: compromised plaintext, explicitly known-plaintext, produces output-ring distance relations between equal plaintext letters in columns separated by complete cycles. It then recovers the input ring from matched plain/cipher. This must not be described as a ciphertext-only recovery.
* The continuation for the improved machine is redacted after printed p. 201 in this release. No missing method was inferred from the redactions.

SSA NR2417, August 1942, pp. 1–4, supplies the useful later-model alternative. Same-day messages share screw settings and identical prepared serial-number beginnings but have different initial ring offsets. Pairing corresponding letters in genuinely isomorphic positions yields constant translations on the unknown output ring. The authors combine these relationships by indirect symmetry, recover an equivalent ring, and choose a decimation by recognizing the keyword-generated ring. Subsequent serial-number deductions, message-opening vocabulary and plaintext assumptions recover input-ring letters and actual screw arcs (pp. 4–9). Those later deductions use traffic conventions and cribs, not pure unconstrained ciphertext statistics. The known-components case (pp. 11–15) likewise uses numeric code conventions and familiar beginnings. Neither German traffic rule nor keyword-generated alphabets are established for Slovak targets.

## Exact transferable indirect symmetry

For messages r and 0 at common prepared positions i, equal plaintext letters and equal lever-press progression imply O(C[r,i]) − O(C[0,i]) = delta[r] modulo 26. O is a permutation of 0..25 and delta[r] is unknown. This relation requires no plaintext letters, input ring, wheel mask or phase as solver input. It applies under either ordinary stepping or selective J as long as the shared prepared prefix includes the same J positions.

`isomorph_solver.py` creates the homogeneous equations, fixes O[A]=0, performs independent Gaussian elimination over GF(2) and GF(13), enumerates the small nullspaces and combines residues by CRT. A valid permutation must have 13 of each parity and two of each mod-13 residue. Complete output rings are then checked for all 26 distinct positions. These are exact constraints; no language model or control truth ranks solutions. Equivalent transformations O -> u*O + b, gcd(u,26)=1, are unavoidable from isomorph constraints alone. The fixed physical wheel residue 23 can later constrain decimation when a supported complete cycle is known, but the solver does not pretend to recover that phase or mask here.

## Fresh genuine control

Independent seed 17952261011 generates a new 26-of-52 active mask, mixed input/output rings, random phase and nine distinct initial offsets. The real verified wheel table gives full selector-cycle displacement 179. Each message has 385 prepared letters; nine messages share a 40-letter prefix and have different following held-out corpus passages. This is a deliberately favorable depth control, not evidence of such depth in HC traffic. The corpus uses only the final 20% of complete Slovak UD sentences; its SHA256 is recorded in control-truth.json. Word separators are explicit J, including any natural J. The encoder advances twice after every plaintext J, once otherwise. Independent inverse derives jumps from unwrapped coordinates rather than the forward arc walk, recovering all nine plaintexts exactly.

The first general integer SMT formulation timed out after ten seconds before producing a model; saved `isomorph-smt-timeout.json`. It was replaced by the finite-field structural reconstruction, not more restarts. The exact method found precisely 12 rings in about 0.006 seconds, all equivalent to the planted output ring and no false rings. Nullity is one over each field. This is output-ring recovery up to affine ambiguity only, not full key or plaintext recovery. Post-search truth comparison appears solely in verification.

Sensitivity checks on 8, 12 and 20 shared positions also gave the same 12 equivalent rings for this nine-message control. Eight positions across nine independent offsets already provide enough connected relationships in this constructed instance; this is not a universal minimum. Changing one ciphertext letter in an assumed shared position reduces both nullspaces to zero and yields no permutation, correctly rejecting the exact false correspondence. Identical cipher prefixes with zero relative shift provide no new ring relationships, so merely observing matching headers across two messages is insufficient.

No historical target ciphertext was processed by this method and no target family was excluded. Applicability requires independently supported isomorphic depth or causal repeats, preferably multiple diverse shifts. The full unknown input ring and physical mask remain unsolved.

## Conditional literal framing check on 350 / 351

Parent requested read-only evaluation of the actual paired framing. Both literal transcriptions have 385 letters. Assuming the entire first 20 positions are equal prepared plaintext and follow equal stepping paths under one common output ring and constant relative initial offset is infeasible: 16 ciphertext identities force zero offset; the four mismatches violate a bijective translation. In particular 350 U (position 7) and J (position 8) both map to 351 U. The last ten similarly have nine identities forcing zero offset and K→M at position 378. Combined first 20 and last ten are also infeasible. These simple contradictions hold for every affine output-ring gauge, without needing enumeration of huge underconstrained spaces.

Using only the identical positions in either window or both imposes delta=0 but gives no output-ring placement information: after O[A]=0, ring nullity remains 25 over GF(2) and GF(13). Those positions are compatible with all arbitrary output-ring permutations; they cannot seed reconstruction. Rank/status and exact positions are in `350-351-framing-conditional.json`; reproduced by `python3 batches/kryha-method-sol61/check_pair.py`.

This rejects only the exact constant-shift/shared-path framing hypothesis for the unchanged literal readings. Possible transcription ambiguities, different stepping paths, different keys, or unencrypted indicators/framing are separate hypotheses. No transcription was corrected or selected to force consistency. Shared ciphertext groups alone do not establish plaintext identities.
